1Work queue replaces hunting
Four counts of everything that needs a human. Each is a saved filter, not a dead statistic — clicking loads exactly those rows with the matching bulk action pre-armed. Problem: staff had to open profiles to discover work. Safeguard: counts are derived server-side so two staff see the same number.
2Six dropdowns → one Filters panel
Person type becomes the primary segmentation (your Athletes / Parents / Account holders language). Everything else — tags, plan, gender, age, custom fields, setup state — moves behind one Filters button with a count, and any combination can be saved as a view.
3Bulk bar that scales past one page
“Select all 218 matching this filter” is the difference between inviting a club and inviting a page. Safeguard: destructive actions (archive, delete) never appear in the bulk bar for staff without the owner-granted permission, and every bulk send shows a skipped-reason summary.
4Setup column shows whose turn it is
Dot + label + 7-segment meter + “waiting on member / waiting on you”. The old readiness vocabulary (Leave alone, Follow-up, Needs card, Hold) collapses into these seven steps, as you asked.
5One recommended action + full menu
The row's button is the single next best action for that person's state. Everything else lives in one ⋯ menu with a consistent order, so “where is Send password reset” has one answer everywhere. Permission-gated items stay visible but locked with the required role named.
6Built for a 5,000-person club
Server-side search, sort and pagination with an A–Z jump; families collapse into one parent row with an expand chevron so a 3-child family costs one line, not four. Counts in the tabs come from the query, not the loaded page.

Who they are, what they pay, whether they can log in, who manages them, where they came from — before any tab is touched. The old header showed a Prospect pill and a phone number.

3Next action, in the profile
Exactly what you asked for: a banner that names the blocker, whose turn it is, how long it's been, and the 1–3 things worth doing. It disappears when there's nothing to do — no permanent decoration.
4Tabs with debt markers
Counts and a red dot mean a tab can advertise a problem (missing waiver) without the owner opening all eleven. Overview stays a summary — every card links into its tab rather than duplicating it.
5Migration lives on the profile
The seven steps with dates and who did them, plus the “nothing has been charged” reassurance in plain language. Staff stop switching between the migration tool and the profile to understand one person.
6Three kinds of ownership, visibly
Pencil = staff editable, arrows = member keeps it current, lock = nobody here. Birthday sits in its own tinted row with the reason and the fix (“Sally updates it in the portal”) rather than a dead disabled input.
7Security is a card, not a mystery
